ADVANCED ULTRASONIC SERVICES

Inspection methods selected for the question at hand.

TOFD, PAUT, TFM and FMC provide different ways to acquire and interpret ultrasonic information. IAIS reviews the component and governing requirements before recommending a procedure and reporting scope.

SERVICES AT A GLANCE

Choose the right evidence for the right decision.

These techniques are related, but they are not interchangeable. TOFD focuses on diffracted signals; PAUT steers and focuses beams electronically; FMC records a full set of transmit-receive combinations; and TFM reconstructs an image from suitable FMC data. Coverage, sensitivity and acceptance criteria are established for each application.

01 / TOFD

Time of Flight Diffraction

TOFD uses a transmitting and receiving probe arrangement to record lateral, back-wall and diffracted signals. It is often used for weld examination and through-wall sizing of suitable indications.

Scope considerations: weld geometry, wall thickness, access on both sides, surface condition, reference blocks and the applicable procedure.

Explore TOFD ↗
02 / PAUT

Phased Array Ultrasonic Testing

PAUT uses multiple probe elements and controlled delays to form, steer or focus ultrasonic beams. Sectorial and other scan views can support weld examination, mapping and indication evaluation where the technique is suitable.

Scope considerations: material, weld profile, probe and wedge selection, calibration, scan plan, encoding and coverage.

Explore PAUT ↗
03 / TFM

Total Focusing Method

TFM reconstructs an ultrasonic image by applying focusing calculations across a defined region of interest. Its use depends on the acquisition setup, material properties, image quality and the project’s evaluation requirements.

Scope considerations: region of interest, probe setup, sound paths, material response, validation and reporting method.

Explore TFM ↗
04 / FMC

Full Matrix Capture

FMC is a data acquisition process in which array elements transmit and receive in combinations. The captured data can be processed for imaging methods such as TFM. FMC describes the acquisition, rather than a stand-alone acceptance result.

Scope considerations: instrument capability, array configuration, data volume, acquisition time and subsequent processing.

PLANNING THE EXAMINATION

What goes into an inspection proposal?

A useful proposal starts with an inspection objective, not just a method name. Combining methods may be appropriate when the approved procedure and technical requirements call for complementary coverage.

01 Component drawings, weld details and dimensions
02 Material, thickness and surface/access conditions
03 Relevant code, specification and acceptance criteria
04 Location, schedule and reporting requirements
Technical note

These explanations describe general method principles. The presence of a technique on this site does not imply suitability for every material or geometry. The project procedure, calibration, personnel and acceptance basis are agreed before execution.
